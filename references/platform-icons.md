# Platform icon requirements

Compiled November 2026 from Apple's and Google's own pages. **Platform rules move every
year. Re-check the linked pages before producing final assets**, and tell the user which
details you confirmed this session and which come from this file. The model's own memory
of icon systems is often a release or two behind; prefer the docs.

How this was verified, so you know how hard to lean on it:
- The Apple guideline and documentation pages were opened directly. They had already
  been revised for the release after iOS 26 (change-log entries in June and October
  2026), so a detail here may describe the newer system rather than iOS 26.
- The WWDC session pages were read through a summariser. Treat points credited to a
  session as paraphrase, not as Apple's wording.
- Not confirmed: whether the automatic two-layer treatment of old bitmap icons also
  happens on iOS 26; the 29, 40 and 60 pt size ladder (recalled from older guidelines and
  no longer printed on the current pages); the 16 px favicon check (recalled).
- The Android and Play pages were opened directly.

## Apple (iOS, iPadOS, macOS)

Sources:
- https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons
- https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/creating-your-app-icon-using-icon-composer
- https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-your-app-icon
- https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass
- WWDC 2025 sessions 220 and 361 on the new icon design and Icon Composer

**Appearances.** The guidelines list six: default, dark, clear light, clear dark, tinted
light, tinted dark. Icon Composer authors three: Default, Dark and Mono.
- Dark uses the same layers with per-appearance fill, opacity and blend overrides.
- Mono is a greyscale version that feeds all four clear and tinted variants. Make the most
  recognisable element white.
- Anything not authored is generated by the system, so author them if the result matters.

**Format.** One `.icon` file holds a background (solid or gradient, set in the tool) plus
foreground layers in at most four groups. Groups are the depth layers. The system adds
the specular highlights, translucency, blur, shadows and the mask.

**What to export.** Flat, opaque SVG layers. No baked shadows, bevels, glows or mask.
Apply fills and gradients inside Icon Composer. Outline any text before export.

**Craft guidance from Apple.**
- Few, simple shapes; filled and overlapping rather than outlined; crisp edges.
- No thin lines and no sharp corners at small scale.
- A simple background. The content does not need to fill the canvas.
- Text only when essential; a single first letter is acceptable.
- Illustrations rather than photos; no replicas of interface elements or Apple hardware.
- The same features in every appearance, and consistency across platforms.

**Canvas.** 1024 x 1024 px, square and unmasked. The system masks it to a rounded
rectangle. No numeric safe area is published: keep the primary content centred and use
Apple's template grid.

**Not updating.** Bitmap icons are still accepted. On newer systems they are split into
two layers automatically and the glass effect is applied only to large simple shapes.
No appearance variant is mandatory for the primary icon, and the layered format is
recommended, not required. Alternate icons, if offered, need their own dark, clear and
tinted variants.

**Older systems.** A `.icon` file replaces the asset-catalog icon and Xcode generates
images for older systems from it. To control the older look, keep an asset catalog with
Any, Dark (transparent background) and Tinted (greyscale).

**Testing.** App Store product page optimisation allows up to three treatments for up to
90 days; alternate icons must be in the binary.
https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/

## Android

Sources:
- https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/launch/icon_design_adaptive
- https://developer.android.com/distribute/google-play/resources/icon-design-specifications

- Adaptive icon: a foreground layer and a background layer, each 108 x 108 dp.
- Keep the logo between 48 and 66 dp. The inner 66 dp is never clipped; the outer 18 dp
  on each side is reserved for the mask and for motion.
- No masks or outline shadows drawn into the layers.
- A monochrome layer is optional and powers themed icons (Android 13 and later). Newer
  releases theme apps that lack one automatically, so supply one to control the result.
- Play listing icon: 512 px, 32-bit PNG, sRGB, at most 1024 KB, full square with no
  shadow or rounding, no ranking or deal text.

## Web

- Favicon: square. Search engines recommend larger than 48 px.
  https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/favicon-in-search
- Still check the mark at 16 px, since that is what a browser tab shows.
- An SVG favicon can carry a dark-mode variant through a media query (recalled, not
  re-checked when this file was compiled; confirm browser support before relying on it).

## Hard constraints versus recommendations

| | Must | Should |
|---|---|---|
| Apple | 1024 px square, unmasked; outlined text in SVG; no Apple hardware; passes review | Ship a `.icon`; at most four groups; flat vector layers; author Dark and Mono; one idea in a few filled shapes; centred content; no words |
| Android | Two layers at 108 dp; logo at most 66 dp; no mask or outline shadow; Play icon to spec | Monochrome layer; vectors; logo at least 48 dp |
| Web | Square favicon | Legible at 16 px; dark variant |

## What this means while drawing

- Draw in **two to four flat filled shapes**. Anything finer is lost at 29 pt and breaks
  the glass treatment.
- Decide early which element turns **white in mono**. If the mark only works through a
  colour contrast, mono will flatten it.
- Keep the mark inside roughly the **central 60%** of the tile (Android's 66 of 108 dp),
  so one drawing survives the iOS mask, Android's circle and squircle masks, and a favicon.
