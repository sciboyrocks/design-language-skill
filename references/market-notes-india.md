# Market notes: India

A worked example of the cultural check (Brief 2 in `research-briefs.md`), compiled
November 2026 for a money product. Read it when the audience is Indian; for any other
market, run the brief fresh and add a sibling file (`market-notes-<market>.md`).

Most rows rest on one or two web sources read as search snippets, so the grades are low
on purpose. Treat this as a list of things to check and questions to ask real users,
not as settled fact.

## Colours

| Colour | Meaning found | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Red | **Auspicious for commerce**: traders' red cloth-bound account books are worshipped at Diwali (Chopda Pujan), mainly among Gujarati traders | MODERATE |
| Red | **Loss**: market reporting uses "in the red" for falls and green for gains, so green is up and red is down (the reverse of China, Japan and Taiwan). No exchange source found | MODERATE |
| Green | Gains; also prosperity and fertility | MODERATE for gains, WEAK otherwise |
| Saffron / orange | Sacred, renunciation; also a party colour since the 1950s | WEAK / MODERATE |
| Gold | Wealth and the goddess Lakshmi; buying gold on Dhanteras is a mass custom | STRONG |
| Yellow as a money colour | No source found | UNSUPPORTED |
| Blue | Generic finance "trust" colour; no India-specific consumer evidence | WEAK |
| Purple | Luxury, spirituality; already owned in payments by a market leader that says it chose purple to stand out | WEAK / MODERATE |
| Terracotta / clay brown | No source on the colour itself; its link to money is the clay money pot | UNSUPPORTED |
| Black | Avoided at traditional wedding ceremonies, loosening in cities | WEAK |
| White | Purity, and also the colour of mourning | WEAK |

Sources: outlooktraveller.com (Chopda Pujan explainer), hdfcsky.com and benzinga.com
(market colour convention), thediplomat.com (party colours), gulfnews.com and
thenationalnews.com (Dhanteras), exchange4media.com (colour psychology in India),
phonepe.com design blog, gktoday.in, color-meanings.com.

**The red trap.** Red is welcome on a cover, a field or a brand mark, and unwelcome on a
number. A red-family brand colour needs a firm rule that amounts are never set in it.

## Symbols of saving and stored wealth

- **Clay money pot (gullak).** The object is known across regions, but the word is
  Hindi/Urdu; Tamil sellers call it "undiyal" and Bangladeshi listings "matir bank"
  (WEAK, retail listings). One newspaper column frames it as a childhood birthday gift,
  so it is nostalgic and child-coded (MODERATE). No negative reading was found, but the
  only usage source was promotional, so that is absence of evidence. Not verified for
  Pakistan or Nepal.
- **Existing associations with the word "gullak".** A long-running Hindi web series about
  middle-class family life, and a venture-backed digital-gold savings app, both use the
  name (STRONG). A new money brand that draws a gullak will be read next to them.
- **Kalash.** A ritual vessel standing for abundance; reads as Hindu religion rather than
  personal saving (MODERATE).
- **Hundi.** Both a temple offering box and a traditional bill of exchange; reads as
  religion or trade credit (STRONG; Reserve Bank of India page on hundies).
- **Potli pouch, tijori safe.** No source found (UNSUPPORTED).
- **Pig-shaped money box.** No controversy about piggy banks was found, and marketplaces
  use "piggy bank" for non-pig pots. Pigs are, however, a live communal flashpoint
  (MODERATE), so treating a pig mascot as risky is a reasonable inference, not a finding.

## Flag and party colours

Saffron, white and green recur on the national flag and on several party flags
(MODERATE). Whether a palette of deep terracotta, very dark green and cream reads as the
tricolour was not answered by any source. A sensible working rule (inference): the risk
is real with an orange-leaning terracotta in equal horizontal bands, and low with a
brown-leaning clay in unequal proportions. Test it on people rather than assuming.

## What to lean on, what to avoid

- Lean on: gold for wealth, green for gain and growth, red for the auspicious ledger.
- Avoid: red on positive amounts, saffron-white-green banding, pig imagery.
- Check with users: whether a clay pot reads as warm or as childish for the intended
  audience, and whether it is confused with existing brands of the same name.
