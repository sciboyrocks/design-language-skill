# Research briefs

Three strands, independent of each other, so run them in parallel when subagents are
available and one after another when they are not. `evidence.md` and
`platform-icons.md` already hold the general findings; fresh research is for what
changes per product: **the market, the category, the culture and this year's platform
rules.**

## Rules for every brief

- **Ask questions, do not hand over a concept.** A researcher given the favoured idea
  returns it confirmed. Ask for disconfirming evidence in so many words.
- **Demand a source and a grade for every claim** (STRONG, MODERATE, WEAK, UNSUPPORTED),
  and ask the researcher to separate what they opened this session from what they recall.
  Drop any statistic that cannot be traced.
- **Keep the user out of the queries.** No names, product names, revenue or other private
  details in web searches. Describe the product generically.
- **Time-box it.** Give a tool-call budget and say that a row marked "recalled, not
  checked" is acceptable. An audit of forty apps with no budget is the kind of brief that
  never comes back; split it or cap it.
- **Ask for a short, structured answer** (word limit, fixed sections) so three reports
  can be compared and merged.

## Brief 1: what is already on the user's screen

Replace the bracketed parts.

```
Time-boxed competitive visual audit for a new [category] [platform] app for [audience]
in [market]. Use web search and open store listings or the companies' own brand pages to
check the CURRENT icon. No personal or private details in search queries.

Hard time box: about 25 tool calls. A row marked RECALLED is acceptable; return what you
have rather than running long. Do not recommend a concept; report what exists.

Part A. For each app give: dominant icon background colour (name and approximate hex),
the symbol on it (letter, wordmark, abstract shape, object), and whether it literally
depicts [the category's subject]. Mark each row VERIFIED or RECALLED.
- Direct competitors: [list 6 to 10]
- Adjacent apps the same person uses daily: [list 6 to 10]
- Global references: [list 4 to 6, RECALLED is fine]

Part B. Summarise:
- Which hue families are crowded, with the apps under each?
- Which are thinly used or empty? Specifically, is [hue you are considering] used?
- Which pictograms already stand for [the association], and who uses them?
- Is there any well-known mark, in any category, built like [describe the incumbent's
  construction in plain geometry]? Name them.

Under 600 words. End with four lines: unoccupied hues; unoccupied pictograms; overused
cliches; marks to avoid resembling.
```

## Brief 2: what colours and symbols mean to this audience

```
Time-boxed cultural check for a branding decision on a [category] product for people in
[market]. Use web search and open sources. General topics only in queries.

Hard time box: about 20 tool calls. Report disconfirming evidence as readily as
confirming evidence. Say plainly when no source could be found.

1. What do these colours connote for this audience, in general and around [the
   category's subject]: [list 6 to 9 hues, including every hue under consideration]?
   Where a colour carries both a positive and a negative meaning, give both.
2. For each symbol under consideration, [list them]: how widely is it recognised across
   regions and generations? Is it in everyday use or mainly nostalgic? Any negative
   connotation? Any prominent brand, show or product already tied to the word or image?
3. Other traditional or everyday symbols of [the association] in this culture, one line
   each on what they connote.
4. Any symbol that is risky here for religious, political or social reasons?
5. Does the palette of [colours] risk reading as a flag, a party or a rival's identity?

Under 650 words, bullets per question, a source URL and a grade on every claim. End with
three lines: meanings safe to lean on; meanings to avoid; which symbols are safe and for
whom.
```

## Brief 3: this year's platform rules

```
Look up the current app icon requirements for [platforms] in the vendors' own
documentation. Do not rely on memory; say when a detail could not be confirmed.

1. Which appearances must or should an icon support, what the vendor calls them, and how
   each is derived.
2. The source format: layers, canvas size, grid, safe area, what the system adds.
3. Explicit craft guidance: simplicity, text, photos, filling the canvas, gradients.
4. What happens to an icon that is not updated to the newest format.
5. Anything that changed in the last 12 months.

Under 600 words, with the URL for each point, and a closing list of hard constraints
("must") versus recommendations ("should").
```

Compare the answer with `platform-icons.md` and trust the fresher one.

## Optional brief: evidence for a new question

Only when the user asks something `evidence.md` does not cover (sound logos, mascots,
motion, naming, a specific category).

```
Focused evidence review on [question]. Read primary sources where possible.

The decision-maker's belief is: "[state it in their words]". I need to know what the
evidence says, not a confirmation of it.

For each finding give the source (author, year, URL you opened), the sample, the effect
with its size or conditions, and a grade. List boundary conditions and failed or missing
replications. Trace any commonly repeated statistic to its origin and say whether it
holds up.

Under 700 words. End with at most five practical implications and one line answering:
what can design do here, and what only use can do?
```
